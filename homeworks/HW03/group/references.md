## Mapping notes

- **Scope boundary rule.** An item earns a place here only if it is a *stage-1* check under the Four Test Stages slide in REF-04: something observable on a rendered screen. Three families are excluded by rule, no matter how good the citation would be: viewport / OS / browser rendering (stage 4, goes to the Task 3 matrix), input-domain coverage such as boundary values (stage 2), and judgements about whether a user understands something (Task 2 probe questions). This rule removed two AI items and one clause of a third in the review pass — see [ai-gap-notes.md](ai-gap-notes.md), Part 2. Apply it before adding an item, not after.
- **REF-04 vs. REF-07 split.** Both are course-slide material, but from two different files. REF-04 (`EN.GUI-Usability-Compatibility-Testing-AI-First.md`) is the only source for the Waghmare per-control checklists. REF-07 (`S13_gui_usability_testing.md`) is the only source for the S13 "Common GUI Bugs", "Checklist Testing", "Navigation Testing", and "Localization" slides. An item is mapped to whichever file it actually appears in, not merged into one entry, so a grader can open the exact slide.
- **Items citing two sources — exactly three.** IA-01-16 (REF-07 S13 Challenges + REF-01 Nielsen #2), IA-02-17 (REF-08 item 7 + REF-04 Text Box), IA-02-20 (REF-07 S13 Bugs + the assignment's own IA-02 aspect label, which is what names the rich-text editor as in scope). Every other row carries a single source. No citation was transferred between items, because the review pass performed no merges: the one citation that moved, Nielsen #2, moved off a removed item onto IA-01-16 rather than from one surviving item to another.
- **Source material still unused.** Two gaps are deliberate, not oversights. REF-04's **Button** control class has no item: every button-level rule the checklist needs is already carried by IA-01-02 (visual hierarchy), IA-01-12 (interactive elements distinguishable) and IA-02-10 (submit disables on first click). REF-08's items 11 (form usable on mobile) and 12 (screen-reader compatibility) have no dedicated row: item 11 is stage-4 compatibility and belongs to the Task 3 matrix; item 12 is covered criterion by criterion under REF-05 (SC 1.1.1, 4.1.2, 4.1.3) rather than as one unfalsifiable "works with a screen reader" row.
- **WCAG share.** REF-05 covers 15 of 61 items (25%), down from 17 of 54 (31%) in the AI draft — SC 3.3.4 Error Prevention was cut as out of scope and one of the two SC 1.4.1 rows was a duplicate. Of the 17 success criteria the generation prompt whitelisted, 15 are used; the two unused are SC 1.4.10 Reflow (banned by the same prompt's constraint on responsive breakpoints, and stage-4 material) and SC 3.3.4. This ratio is worth watching: the assignment grades against heuristics and per-widget checklists, and a checklist where a third of the rows cite a standard the course deck never teaches by criterion number reads as a WCAG audit rather than a GUI review. The `Human` pass moved it in the right direction by adding per-control and ISTQB coverage instead of more accessibility criteria.
- **Nielsen vs. Shneiderman overlap on feedback / error handling.** Several items could cite either Nielsen #1 (Visibility of system status) or Shneiderman #3 (Offer informative feedback) — the same UX principle from two authors. The checklist follows one convention: Nielsen for *state visibility* items (loading indicators, progress bars — IA-01-05, IA-04-02), Shneiderman for *action-outcome* items (toast or confirmation after a completed action — IA-04-01), so no row cites both.
- **No item cites REF-06 as a real source.** It is a bookkeeping row for the 3 items the assignment explicitly allows to have no external citation ("EMS-specific"). Do not add a 4th, external "TBD" source here; if a genuinely new external source is consulted later, give it its own Ref ID instead of overloading REF-06.
- **`EMS` root cause and "EMS-specific" citation are two different things.** A `Human` item can be `EMS`-caused in [ai-gap-notes.md](ai-gap-notes.md) — the AI could not have written it without seeing the running system — and still cite an external source, because the *rule* it checks is documented even though the *trigger* was found on the SUT. IA-01-16 is the one case: `EMS` root cause, but listed under REF-07 and REF-01, not REF-06.
